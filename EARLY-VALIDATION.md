# Early Validation

A running list of things worth prototyping and getting real runtime on *before* a design decision that depends on them is treated as settled — mechanisms with enough technical risk (fragile external tooling, easy-to-misconfigure setup, environment-dependent behavior) that discussing them on paper isn't enough confidence. Different from [Open questions](TRACKING.md#open-questions): those are undecided design questions; these are decisions we've made but haven't yet proven out in practice.

Each entry should say what to validate, why it's risky, and what "validated" would look like.

Also different from [Backlog](TRACKING.md#backlog), which holds decisions not yet *built*: the question here is whether a mechanism works in practice, not whether it exists. A mechanism may legitimately appear in both — built there, proven here — and `git`'s merge driver currently does. **Keep the build note out of an entry here.** The correction to the re-embedding entry below is what that failure looks like: a mechanism worth building was recorded as a caveat inside a validation result, which is not a place anyone looks for work, and it sat unbuilt for a week.

- [ ] **Whether the rewritten `context` description actually changes what gets written there.** The description shipped 2026-09-04 on the argument that a field's own description is what drives its contents — evidenced by the previous wording, which produced 98% adoption of an optional field and returned its own vocabulary. That argument makes the *new* wording a prediction, and an unusually testable one, because the corpus is the instrument and the baseline was measured the same day.

  **The baseline, across both shared sets, live memories only:** 86 of 88 `scatterhoard` and 89 of 91 `coding-lessons` carry a context, median 240 characters. Of those contexts, 89% and 65% carry a date; 82% and 73% carry occasion phrasing; **72% and 62% carry a checkability pointer**, which is the drift the rewrite targets. Of 135 dated contexts, 103 sat within a day of `created.at`.

  **Validated looks like:** contexts written after this date carry activities rather than identifiers, dates fall away without anything else being lost, and pointers move into `content`/`why` rather than simply disappearing. **The failure to watch for is the opposite of the old one** — a description that now enumerates exclusions could produce contexts that are merely shorter, naming an activity so thin it jogs nothing. Median length falling a long way with no gain in specificity would be that.

  **Re-measure with the same method** — the classification is a handful of regexes over the exported files, and the honest comparison needs contexts written under the new wording only, so it wants a few sessions' worth rather than the next one. Note the confound this cannot escape and should not pretend to: the same model that wrote the description writes the contexts, which is [the subject-and-experimenter problem](TRACKING.md#open-questions) named in the convention-measurement question.

- [x] **`model2vec-rs` seed quality on realistic memory text.** *Validated 2026-07-31, with caveats.* On a 45-memory labelled corpus of real Scatterhoard design memories with 20 queries, `potion-base-32M` reached 77.5% recall@5 and 87.5% recall@10 with centroid removal — ahead of `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` at r@5 and within a point at r@10 on the identical corpus and metric. The published MTEB gap does not appear on this workload; the ceiling is the task, not the model. Also established: embedding content+why+keywords beats content alone by 14 points at r@5, confirming the [Recall Mechanisms](documents/design/recall-mechanisms.md) decision empirically. Caveat: one small single-domain corpus, written and labelled by the same author, so absolute numbers are soft even though the model-to-model comparison is sound. Worth re-running against a real memory set once one exists.
- [x] **Re-embedding cost on the import path.** *Validated 2026-07-31.* ~18,000 memories/sec for `potion-base-32M`, so a full cold re-embed of a 10,000-memory set is comfortably sub-second. The content-hash incremental cache remains worth building but is not load-bearing for acceptable import latency. **Correction, 2026-08-08: that last sentence filed the cache under its weakest benefit and it sat unbuilt for a week as a result.** The cache is a *correctness* mechanism, not a speed one — keying a vector by the hash of the text it was derived from is what makes a stale vector unrepresentable rather than merely avoided, and what makes "which memories need embedding" a query instead of an assumption. Three defects trace to its absence: `no-record-of-which-model-built-the-vectors`, `two-servers-can-disagree-about-the-model`, and `a-populated-store-cannot-gain-embeddings`. Measuring away the reason something was listed is not the same as establishing it is not needed — the same trap [embeddings.md](documents/design/embeddings.md) avoids deliberately when it lists hubness *last* among the reasons for partitioning by scope, so that the decision survives its weakest argument evaporating.

- [ ] **Whether traversal is observable at all below the corpus crossover.** Exercised 2026-08-19 against the live 44-memory set, and the answer so far is *only after paging*. Four queries produced **no `inherited` at all in their first tranche**: five seeds already cover most of a topical cluster at this size, so both ends of an authored edge tend to be direct hits, and a walk origin never inherits. Traversal appeared only when the first tranche's ids were passed back as `already_known` — the second tranche then carried two reached memories at `inherited` 0.186, one with an own similarity of **0.054**, present entirely because it is linked to the memory the tranche led with. **This is the project's own crossover prediction arriving in practice:** the linked-target percentile measurement put the crossover near 170 memories, and the live set is a quarter of that. Two consequences worth carrying. The walk is not idle below the crossover, it is *redundant* — it reaches memories the direct hits already covered. And `already_known` turns out to be the only way to see traversal work on a small set, which is a use for it nobody designed: it was built for context saving and stateless paging. Validated when: the set passes roughly 170 memories and first-tranche results start carrying `inherited` without paging. Until then, judging whether a reached memory is *worth its context* — SETUP.md §5 item 16 — can only be done a tranche down. On the one sample available, one of the two reached memories was clearly useful and the other tangential. Note that `already_known` is the escape hatch either way — a cut that is too small is one call from being larger — so the risk here is a caller that does not page, not a caller that cannot.
- [ ] **CSLS local scaling at realistic corpus size.** Hubness was surfaced by the spike above (worst hub 13 of 20 queries under `potion-base-32M`; MiniLM 8 of 20, so not a static-embedding artifact but a general high-dimensional one). Centroid removal cuts it to 6 of 20 and is adopted. CSLS was tested as a further correction and **not adopted**: alone it is worse than centroid removal, and stacked on top it buys +0.8 points at r@5 and one hub for a 7.5-point regression at r@3. Risk in that conclusion: k=10 over a 45-memory corpus computes the penalty across ~22% of the entire set, which is nothing like k=10 over several thousand memories — the measurement may simply not transfer. Validated when: re-run against a memory set of realistic size, at which point CSLS is either adopted or ruled out on evidence that applies. Note the interaction with the lowered `store-when` threshold, which invites more structural memories sharing subsystem vocabulary and so more hub pressure.

- [ ] **`SUPERSEDED_BY` against a two-scope corpus.** Adopted on structural grounds — it is how the lower-authority side records its own obsolescence without an imported memory reaching across the trust boundary (see [Cross-Set References](documents/design/cross-set-references.md)). - [ ] **The service gate's refusal in ordinary use.** Built 2026-08-30 (`correction-gated-on-service`): amending a record that has been exported anywhere is refused, and the caller supersedes instead. The design traced the consequences it could see and accepted them, but two things can only be observed in use. **How often the refusal is *right***: the corpus measurement behind the decision found both existing corrections landing within 144 seconds of the memory being written, which is well inside the window before any export — if that holds, the gate should almost never fire, and a gate that fires often means the measurement did not generalise. And **whether the refusal teaches**: it arrives at the moment a session is trying to repair something, which is the worst moment to be told to do it differently, so what matters is whether the session supersedes cleanly on the next call or thrashes. Validated when: a stretch of real sessions is looked at for how many amendments were refused, what each was trying to fix, and what the session did next. Note the asymmetry to watch for — a `local` set with no backup never gates at all, so the refusal rate says nothing about it.

  **First two observations, both 2026-08-30 and both from a client.** One was a *probe* — an amendment attempted in order to see the refusal, which says the guard fires and nothing about need. The other was real: a recalibration of `01M03V6ZSKFP9B2SADH7T1VTWR` in `coding-lessons` from 0.85 to 0.9, wanted because the memory predicted that a redundant-looking line would one day become load-bearing and that had just happened. **It was refused, and the session dropped the change rather than superseding** — a supersession would have put a second copy of an unchanged claim in the chain for a 0.05 move, which is the trade the corpus measurement already described. Risk: registering a merge driver requires per-clone local `git` config, which is the same class of mechanism as `git` hooks — known to have mixed reliability getting them to actually fire. Validated when: the driver reliably registers itself (ideally via Scatterhoard's own installer, not a manual step) and reliably fires across a handful of real clones/machines/`git` versions, on both a clean conflict (should field-merge silently) and a genuine same-field conflict (should fall through to a real conflict for the LLM/human) — with confirmation that when it *doesn't* fire (unregistered driver), the fallback to plain `git` merge behaves as the safe default described in the design, not silently.
